There was a time when Mac users could identify an application almost immediately. Not by its icon. By how it felt. The scrolling. The typography. The menus. The keyboard shortcuts.

Every detail reflected the operating system around it. The software belonged.

## Consistency Is A Feature

Consistency is often dismissed as cosmetic. It isn't. When applications share common behaviours, users spend less time learning. Knowledge transfers naturally. One application's menu structure helps explain another.

One keyboard shortcut reinforces the next. The platform becomes easier to navigate. The user becomes more confident.

## Software As Craft

Native applications were often built by people deeply familiar with their chosen platform. The software reflected that understanding. Attention was paid to details that never appeared on marketing pages. Text rendering. Window management. Accessibility. Performance.

Small decisions accumulated into a larger feeling. The feeling that the software was designed rather than assembled.

## Why People Still Notice

Despite enormous changes in computing, users continue to notice quality. They notice responsiveness. They notice polish. They notice when an application respects platform conventions. Not because they consciously analyse those decisions. Because good design feels effortless.

The user rarely identifies the reason. They simply prefer using the software. And that preference matters.
