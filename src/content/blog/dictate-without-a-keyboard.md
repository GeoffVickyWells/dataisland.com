Plenty of people can speak a second or third language passably, or know someone who can: a colleague, a friend, a family member. Very few of them want to memorize a Japanese IME or an Arabic keyboard layout for the sake of a single email. Typing was never really the requirement; getting the right words into the document was.

macOS Dictation removes the input problem entirely. Speak, and the words appear already in the correct script (right-to-left, accented, tone marks and all) without adding a keyboard layout you'll never use again. It transcribes speech straight into FusionMD or PolyWrite. And for the lines that really need to land right, speaking them yourself skips the layer where machine translation tends to introduce mistakes.

## Turn on Dictation for the language you need

In **System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation**, turn Dictation on and set the language you'll be speaking in. If you need to switch languages between messages, that's where you switch it.

In **FusionMD** or **PolyWrite**, click into the document where you want the text, then press <kbd>Fn</kbd> <kbd>Fn</kbd> (or choose **Edit → Start Dictation**) and speak. The words land in the document exactly like typed text, in the same font and the same paragraph, ready for spell-check, formatting and export like anything else you wrote.

## Why speaking beats translating, for the parts that matter

Our [Writing for International Trade](/tutorials/international-trade) guide covers writing in English or French and using on-device translation to convert it to your partner's language. That's fast, and good enough for routine correspondence. But a translation is still a second-hand version of what you meant, generated by a model guessing at your intent. Idiom, tone and register are exactly the things machine translation still gets subtly wrong, and if you don't read the target language well, you have no way to catch it before you send it.

Dictation skips that layer. If you, a bilingual colleague, or a native-speaking contact on the phone can just say the sentence in the target language, what lands in the document is the actual sentence someone meant, not a translation's best guess at it.

> **This isn't an argument against translation.** For an entire document in a language you don't speak a word of, translation is still the only practical option. Dictation is for the handful of lines (a closing statement, a specific offer, an apology, a condolence) where getting the exact words right matters more than getting the whole document done fast.

## Use both together

Draft the bulk of a document in your working language and machine translate it as covered in [Writing for International Trade](/tutorials/international-trade). Then dictate directly over the specific lines that need to be exact, yourself or with a native speaker on the line reading it back to you. Select the translated sentence in FusionMD or PolyWrite, start Dictation, and speak the replacement. There's no re-formatting needed; it drops straight into the existing paragraph.

## Who this is for

- **Anyone messaging family or contacts abroad.** Write a real letter home without fumbling accented characters or a script you've never typed before.
- **Businesses closing a deal,** where the exact wording of one line (a figure, an offer, a commitment) matters more than the rest of the document.
- **Writers including authentic dialogue or quotes.** Dictate a line in another language the way a native speaker would actually say it, not the way a translator guesses it.
- **Anyone with a bilingual friend or colleague on hand.** Hand them the microphone instead of asking them to proofread a translation after the fact.

Your words, in their language, said the way you'd actually say them. No new keyboard layout to learn, and no cloud translation service required for the sentences that need to be exact.
